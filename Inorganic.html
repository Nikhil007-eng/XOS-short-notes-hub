Chapter One
Periodic Table & Periodicity
IUPAC Naming (Z > 100)

Elements 101–103 are Mendelevium (Md), Nobelium (No), and Lawrencium (Lr).

If element 119 is discovered, it will be named Ununennium (Uue) and will be an alkali metal in Group 1.

Group 13–18 elements (Z = 113–118):

"नेहा फ्लाई टू मॉस्को टू लिव इन 10 अगेन"

→ Nh (113) · Fl (114) · Mc (115) · Lv (116) · Ts (117) · Og (118)

Atomic Radius

Radius generally increases down the group. Exception: Al > Ga due to the poor shielding of 3d electrons in Gallium.

Due to Lanthanide Contraction (poor shielding of 4f electrons), 4d and 5d series radii become nearly identical:

Zr ≈ Hf Nb ≈ Ta Mo ≈ W Ag ≈ Au
Ionization Energy (IE)

Boron Family "W" graph:

B > Tl > Ga > Al > In

Group 14: Pb > Sn

3D series IE order:

"जान फिर कोई बात क्यों नहीं मानती क्राइंग इज वेरी गुड स्कैम"

Zn > Fe > Co > Cu > Ni > Mn > Cr > V > Ti > Sc

Electron Affinity (EA)

Chlorine has the highest (most negative) electron affinity — not Fluorine!

Endothermic (positive EA): Noble gases, N, Be, Mg, and all anions. Neon has the highest positive EA.

Halogens (magnitude):

Cl> F> Br> I

Chalcogens (magnitude):

S> Se> Te> Po> O
Nature of Oxides
  • Neutral: CO, NO, N₂O
  • Acidic: High OS metals (+6, +7) like CrO₃, Mn₂O₇; non-metal oxides
  • Amphoteric:

    "आज सब कार वेंटो 13" → As³⁺, Sb³⁺, Cr³⁺, V⁵⁺, Te⁴⁺

    "जान सुनो बे आलिया पब गई"

    ZnO · SnO · BeO · Al₂O₃ · PbO

Chemical Bonding
Bond order = 0 means the molecule does not exist. MOT Principle

Fajans' Rule: covalent character increases with a larger anion and a smaller, highly charged cation. LiI is more covalent than LiF.

Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT)

Dipole Moment & Boiling Point Order

A highly tested sequence — memorise this exactly:

H₂O> HF> NH₃> HCl> CHCl₃> H₂S> HBr> HI> NF₃
Fajans' Rule — Covalent Character
  • Larger anion → more covalent character
  • Smaller, highly charged cation → more covalent
  • LiI > LiF in covalent character
  • High ionic potential = distortion = more covalent

  • Secondary valency = Coordination number

Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

Octahedral: Splits into t₂g (lower) and eg (higher). eg contains dx²-y² and dz².

Square Planar energy order:

dx²-y² > dxy > dz² ≈ dxz ≈ dyz

Spectrochemical Series:

C-donors > N-donors > O-donors > Halogens

Exception: For Co³⁺, O-donors also act as strong-field ligands. For 4d and 5d series — all ligands act as strong field.

04
Chapter Four
p-Block Elements
Inert Pair Effect

Going down Groups 13, 14, and 15, the lower oxidation state becomes more stable because ns² electrons are reluctant to participate in bonding.

Tl⁺¹>Tl⁺³ Pb²⁺>Pb⁴⁺ Bi³⁺>Bi⁵⁺

Consequence: Pb⁴⁺ is a strong oxidizing agent (wants to become Pb²⁺).

Bond Energy: Halogens
"काला बंदर फिर इंडिया में"

Cl₂ > Br₂ > F₂ > I₂

F₂ has a lower bond energy than Cl₂ due to lone pair repulsion in the small F atom.

Boiling Point Rules
  • Groups 13 & 14 BP decreases down the group
  • Groups 15–18 BP increases down the group
  • Gallium: lowest MP in Gr.13 but very high BP
Hydride BP Order (H-bonding)

Group 16 exception:

H₂O> H₂Te> H₂Se> H₂S

Group 15:

SbH₃> NH₃> AsH₃> PH₃
Group 15 Hydrides
  • Basic strength ↓ down group: NH₃ is the strongest base
  • Reducing power ↑ down group: BiH₃ is the strongest reducing agent

d & f Block Elements
Oxidation States
  • Sc only +3
  • Zn only +2
  • Mn highest in 3d = +7
  • Ru, Os highest in entire d-block = +8
Melting Points & Atomization
  • W (Tungsten) — highest melting point
  • Mn and Zn have unexpectedly low MP (stable half/full d-configs limit metallic bonding)
  • Highest heat of atomization in 3d: Vanadium (V)

Potassium Dichromate (K₂Cr₂O₇)

From Chromite ore (FeCr₂O₄): Fuse with Na₂CO₃ + O₂ → Na₂CrO₄ (Yellow) → acid → Na₂Cr₂O₇ → +KCl → K₂Cr₂O₇ (Orange).

CrO₄²⁻ (Yellow) ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ (Orange): Acid shifts yellow → orange; Base shifts orange → yellow.

Lanthanides & Actinides
  • Key configs: Ce (4f¹5d¹6s²), Gd (4f⁷5d¹6s²), Lu (4f¹⁴5d¹6s²)
  • Mischmetall: ~95% Lanthanide + ~5% Fe — used in bullets & lighter flints
  • Promethium (Pm) — only radioactive lanthanide
  • All actinides are radioactive. Highest OS = +7 (Np, Pu)

Interstitial Compounds — Quick Facts

Small non-metals (H, B, C, N) trapped in metal lattice voids. Properties: chemically inert · very hard · high melting points · retain metallic conductivity.
